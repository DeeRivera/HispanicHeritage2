Hispanic Heritage Month

Home Topic Resources

From September 15 to October 15, Hispanics across the United States celebrate a month dedicated to independence, culture, and history. What began as a week long celebration in 1968 expanded into a full month in 1988. The celebration starts on the 15th of September because many Hispanic countries including Guatemala, Honduras, Costa Rica, El Salvador, and Nicaragua celebrate their independence the same day. Mexico celebrates their independence on the 16th of September and Chile celebrates their independence on the 18 of September.

Did you know?

  • Hispanic music has influenced America tremendously. Major collaborations like the Drake and Romeo Santos' track "Odio", as well as Bad Bunny's historic Superbowl performance. Genres like salsa, bachata, reggaeton continue to contribute to the sound of American music.
  • Hispanics are completely entrenched in American sports. Mexican-American boxer Ryan Garcia holds the WBC belt at welter weight. Hispanic athletes have made great contributions across American sport teams like baseball, soccer, and football.
  • Hispanics traditions enrich communities across America. Cinco De Mayo, Dia de los Muertos, and the influence on Halloween shows how traditions blend throughout the United States. Hispanic foods like tacos, empanadas, and arroz con pollo have become huge staples all around United States.

Celebrating Heritage

Hispanic Heritage Month is a time to honor the rich culture and contributions of Hispanic Americans. It's a celebration of the diverse traditions, arts, and achievements that have shaped the United States.
